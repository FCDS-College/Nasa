Summary, in NASA's words

Verbatim from the investigation's PSI ISA-Tab metadata. Each section links to its evidence row.

Objective

[[icon:FileText:12]] source

The Flame Extinguishment Experiment (FLEX) investigates the burning and extinction of isolated droplets of liquid fuel in microgravity. The objective is to determine how oxygen concentration, diluent type and ambient pressure affect the extinction of spherical droplet flames, and to identify the conditions in which flames can no longer be sustained.

Description

[[icon:FileText:12]] source

Fuel droplets of heptane and methanol are deployed on fibers inside the Multi-user Droplet Combustion Apparatus in the Combustion Integrated Rack aboard the International Space Station. Each droplet is ignited and allowed to burn in a controlled oxygen, nitrogen, carbon dioxide and helium mixture while video and radiometer data are recorded.

Relevance to spacecraft fire safety

[[icon:FileText:12]] source

Results define the limiting oxygen index of droplet flames in low-gravity environments and help set the fire-suppression requirements of future spacecraft.

Findings from this investigation's data

Lower oxygen, more self-extinguishing droplet flames

[[icon:Calculator:12]] Measured

In FLEX, 82% of 38 droplet flames at O₂ below 0.16 ended in extinction, versus 11% of 71 at O₂ of 0.30 or more.

  • Raw shares: pressure, diluent and droplet size also differ between these groups; the outcome model separates their effects.
  • Evidence lists a sample of the tests' recorded end states (4 per group).

Reduced-pressure droplet tests

[[icon:Calculator:12]] Measured

24 FLEX tests ran at or below 76 kPa (exploration-like pressure); 54% ended in extinction, versus 33% of 142 tests near 1 atm.

  • Oxygen fraction was not held constant between these groups.
  • Evidence lists a sample of the tests' recorded end states (4 per group).

Outcome model estimate for this atmosphere

[[icon:FlaskConical:12]] Model estimate
