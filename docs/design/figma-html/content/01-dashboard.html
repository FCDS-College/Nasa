Which NASA microgravity fire experiments speak to your spacecraft atmosphere?

Moon and Mars vehicles plan to run at lower pressure and higher oxygen than the ISS, which makes materials burn more readily. Pick a cabin atmosphere: Flame in Freefall ranks NASA's flight investigations by how directly their data covers it, states what the flight tests measured there (each figure linked to the source row), estimates droplet-flame outcomes with a validated model, and says plainly what the data cannot tell you.

Measured = computed from flight-test rows · Model estimate = validated FLEX model · Coverage gap = not covered. Click a test ID to see its source row.

Lower oxygen, more self-extinguishing droplet flames

[[icon:Calculator:12]] Measured

In FLEX, 82% of 38 droplet flames at O₂ below 0.16 ended in extinction, versus 11% of 71 at O₂ of 0.30 or more.

  • Raw shares: pressure, diluent and droplet size also differ between these groups; the outcome model separates their effects.
  • Evidence lists a sample of the tests' recorded end states (4 per group).

Reduced-pressure droplet tests

[[icon:Calculator:12]] Measured

Outcome model estimate for this atmosphere

[[icon:FlaskConical:12]] Model estimate

For a 3.00 mm droplet with no added diluent at 0.21 O₂ and 101.3 kPa, the FLEX outcome model gives Heptane: 55% burned to completion, 31% self-extinguished, 14% droplet disruption; Methanol: 48% burned to completion, 40% self-extinguished, 12% droplet disruption.

  • Model flex-outcome-rf-1.2.0: macro-F1 0.71 (baseline 0.29), extinction ROC-AUC 0.89; droplets only.

What this flight data cannot tell you

[[icon:AlertTriangle:12]] Coverage gap

Every flight test here ran in microgravity; none ran at lunar (1/6 g) or Martian (3/8 g) gravity, so partial-gravity flame behaviour is not covered. BASS-II records test conditions but no outcome in its tables.
