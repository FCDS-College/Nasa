How does oxygen fraction affect flame spread in microgravity?
In microgravity, flame spread over solid fuels is strongly governed by oxygen concentration and by the (low) opposed-flow velocity. Increasing the oxygen fraction from 21% to 30% raises the measured spread rate and widens the flammable regime in BASS-II samples [1].
At low flow velocities the flame can quench through radiative heat loss, so a higher oxygen fraction is needed to sustain spread than in normal gravity; the effect is largest below roughly 5 cm/s [2]. Soot volume fraction also increases because the lack of buoyant acceleration yields wider flames and longer residence times [3].

What about CO2 and helium diluents in FLEX droplet tests?
In FLEX, replacing nitrogen with CO2 raises the limiting oxygen index for droplet flame extinction because of CO2's higher heat capacity and radiative absorption, while helium lowers it through higher thermal diffusivity [1].
Consistent with the earlier discussion of oxygen fraction, extinction in these tests was radiative rather than blow-off driven [2].
